Promulgated under Article 123(1) of the Constitution ("Parliament is not in session and the
President is satisfied that circumstances exist which render it necessary for him to take
immediate action"). Signed **Rajendra Prasad, President**; countersigned K. V. K. Sundaram,
Secretary to the Government of India. Repealed by section 12 of Act VIII of 1952, which

## Full text of the operative sections

[Full text as recovered — see below]

---

An Ordinance further to amend the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947

1. Short title and commencement.

2. Amendment of section 2, Act VII of 1947 — clause (o) omitted.

3. Amendment of section 8, Act VII of 1947 — sub-section (3) omitted.

4. Amendment of section 12, Act VII of 1947 — "prohibit the export" replaced by "prohibit the
taking or sending out by land, sea or air (hereafter in this section referred to as export)".

5. Substitution of new section 18 — expanded "certain provisions as to companies" covering
foreign-controlled companies, dividend/asset-realisation directions, and (sub-section 3) a new
restriction: "no person resident in the States shall do any act whereby a company which is
controlled by persons resident in India ceases to be so controlled" without RBI permission —
and (sub-section 4) restricting loans to foreign-controlled companies.

6. Amendment of section 19 — broadened Central Government/RBI power to demand information,
books or documents from any person.

7. Amendment of section 23 — RBI empowered to act "by a general or special order".

8. Insertion of new sections 23A and 23B —
   - **23A** applies the **Sea Customs Act, 1878** to FERA violations (restrictions under ss.8(1)(2),
     12(1), 13(1)(a) "deemed to have been imposed under section 19 of the Sea Customs Act"),
     with s.183 modified so seizure/confiscation is discretionary ("may" for "shall").
   - **23B** creates an **attempts** offence: "Whoever attempts to contravene any of the provisions
     of this Act... shall be deemed to have contravened that provision."

9. Amendment of section 24 — adds a **reverse burden of proof**: where a person is prosecuted for
contravening s.4(3), "the burden of proving that the foreign exchange acquired by such person
has been used for the purpose for which permission to acquire it was granted shall be on him."

10. Insertion of new section 24A — a **presumption as to documents**: documents furnished or
seized under s.19(2) are presumed true and presumed to be in the handwriting/signature they
purport to bear, unless the accused proves otherwise.

## Why this matters for the essay

This is a substantial **enforcement-power tightening** of FERA passed by Ordinance in December
1951 — six months before the Act returned to Parliament as Act VIII of 1952 (the *ultra vires*
correction), and nearly six years before the 1957 permanence amendment. It shows a pattern of
executive action strengthening FERA's teeth (reverse burden of proof, document presumptions,
an attempts offence, Sea Customs Act application) that predates and is independent of the
1957 sterling-agreement expiry — useful for the essay's argument that FERA's trajectory toward
permanence was already administratively underway well before 1957, driven by prosecutorial and
enforcement concerns rather than by the sterling settlement specifically.

**No mention of Britain, sterling, or the sterling balances anywhere in the Ordinance** — consistent
with every other operative legal instrument recovered in this project. This document is entirely
about domestic enforcement mechanics.
