## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

> **Experience has shown that consistently with the requirements of the Second Five Year Plan, it is not possible for the Reserve Bank to comply with the requirements of section 33(2) of the Reserve Bank of India Act in regard to the size of the foreign securities holdings of its Issue Department, notwithstanding that temporary and partial relaxation of the requirement is available under section 37 of the Act. Following the practice adopted by many advanced countries, it is considered advantageous to introduce a greater measure of flexibility in regard to the composition of the currency reserve, provided a certain minimum of gold holding is maintained. The Reserve Bank of India (Amendment) Ordinance, 1957 (6 of 1957), was promulgated on the 31st October, 1957, for the aforesaid purpose. This Bill seeks to replace the Ordinance.**
>
> New Delhi;
> **The 8th November, 1957.**
>
> **T. T. KRISHNAMACHARI.**
>
> M. N. KAUL, Secretary.

## What these two documents establish

1. **The date is 31 October 1957, and the instrument was an Ordinance, not an Act.** The Rs 200 crore / Rs 115 crore floor was imposed by **Presidential Ordinance No. 6 of 1957, promulgated 31 October 1957**, expressly because **"Parliament is not in session and the President is satisfied that circumstances exist which render it necessary for him to take immediate action."** The Act (No. 48 of 1957, the *Second* Amendment Act) came later and was **retrospective to 31 October 1957**, merely replacing the Ordinance. This is why the consolidated Act text carries "w.e.f. 31-10-1957" — a fact T1.7 correctly identified from the footnotes but could not previously explain.

2. **The stated reason is the Second Five Year Plan, in the Government's own words, signed by the same minister who moved FERA permanence ten weeks earlier.** *"[I]t is not possible for the Reserve Bank to comply with the requirements of section 33(2)... in regard to the size of the foreign securities holdings of its Issue Department, notwithstanding that temporary and partial relaxation of the requirement is available under section 37."*

3. **Both escape hatches were exhausted simultaneously.** Section 37 (the temporary-suspension mechanism, which Act 38 of 1956 had itself amended on 6 October 1956 — see T1.7) is described here as already inadequate: the relaxation available under it was "temporary and partial" and still insufficient. So s.3 of the Ordinance rewrote the floor itself **and** s.4 deleted the proviso to s.37 — i.e. the Government both lowered the bar and removed a limit on how long it could stay below the old one, in the same instrument, by emergency decree.

4. **The sequence is now fully dated and continuous:**
   - **26 July 1957** — FERA (Amendment) Bill No. 47 introduced; SOR cites foreign-exchange shortage "likely to continue for an indefinite period" and the Second Five Year Plan (see `GAZETTE_1957-07-26_FERA_Amendment_Bill_47_of_1957_SOR_FULLTEXT.md`).
   - **31 August 1957** — FERA permanence passed by Lok Sabha in a single sitting.
   - **31 October 1957** — RBI currency-reserve floor rewritten by Presidential Ordinance, Parliament not in session, immediate effect.
   - **8 November 1957** — TTK signs the SOR conceding the Reserve Bank *cannot comply* with the existing law.
   - **13 November 1957** — replacement Bill No. 80 introduced, retrospective to 31 October.

   Ten weeks separate the permanence of exchange control from the emergency rewriting of the currency-backing law. Both instruments name the Second Five Year Plan as the cause. **Neither names Britain, sterling, or the sterling balances.**

5. **Correction to the project's prior working assumption, now on primary evidence.** The commonly repeated claim that "in 1956 the Proportional Reserve System was replaced by the Minimum Reserve System" is not supported by these documents for the Rs 200cr/Rs 115cr figures. Act 38 of 1956 (6 October 1956) amended other provisions; the reserve floor itself was set on **31 October 1957**, by Ordinance. The essay must use the 1957 date.
