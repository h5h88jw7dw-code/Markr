# GATT L-series document index — India's 1957 filings, with direct links

**Source:** `https://www.wto.org/gatt_docs/l.htm` — the WTO's own index of GATT `L/`-series
documents, complete with date, title, and direct PDF link for each. This resolves the project's
"GATT 1957 India consultation" target — the index itself was the missing piece; individual
documents are one click each.

| **L/719** | 23 Oct 1957 | CONTRACTING PARTIES - Twelfth Session - Indian Import Restrictions - Statement by the Government of India on the Licensing Policy for the Period October 1957 and March 1958 | `https://www.wto.org/gatt_docs/English/SULPDF/90710030.pdf` — **fetched and saved in full, and this is the standout document of the whole pass**: dated 8 days before the RBI Ordinance (31 Oct 1957), it states outright that reserves "stand substantially below the minimum prescribed by law" (Rs 4,000 million) — see `GATT_L719_1957-10-23_India_Statement_Licensing_Policy_FULLTEXT.md`. |

**Two standout finds among these.** L/648/Add.1 (6 Aug 1957) is India's own explanation to GATT,
three weeks before FERA permanence, explicitly naming "sterling balances" as the form of India's
declining reserves (Rs.4,317 million on 19 July 1957 vs. Rs.7,353 million eighteen months earlier).
**L/719 (23 Oct 1957) is better still** — eight days before the RBI Ordinance, it states outright
that reserves have fallen below the statutory floor. See both dedicated writeups for the important
nuance on how "sterling balances" is being used (accounting terminology, not a claim of British
constraint).

## How to use the index for anything else

`https://www.wto.org/gatt_docs/l.htm` lists every `L/` document with date and title in one long
page — searchable by browser Ctrl-F for "India," "Indian," or any other term. The parallel `BOP/`
and other series indexes (if this project needs India's later Article XVIII:B balance-of-payments
consultations, which per an earlier search began "regularly since 1957") are likely at neighboring
URLs on `wto.org/gatt_docs/` — not checked this pass, worth a follow-up.
