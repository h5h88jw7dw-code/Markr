# Political context for the speakers in the Rajya Sabha, 6 September 1957, FERA debate

Not for the essay — for weighing why each speaker said what they said. Sourced from Wikipedia,
Nehru Memorial archive, and eParlib's own "Eminent Parliamentarians" monograph series.

## Shri Bhupesh Gupta — the one who raised sterling ("forced accumulation", "the mischief of the British")

**Communist Party of India, Rajya Sabha, West Bengal, 1952–1981** (elected 13 May 1952, sat until
his death — longest-serving RS member at the time). Born 20 October 1914, Mymensingh District,
Bengal. Joined the **Anushilan Samiti** (Bengal revolutionary underground) young; arrested 1930 and
1931; detained 1933–? for freedom-movement activity. Read for the Bar at Middle Temple, London,
and while in England was active in the **India League** alongside Krishna Menon — where he was a
close personal friend of Indira Gandhi, despite later political distance (per *The New Indian
Express*, "Indira Wanted Soviets On Board For The Emergency"). Died 1981 in Moscow.

**What this explains:** Gupta's "forced accumulation" language and "undo the mischief of the
British" is not a stray rhetorical flourish — it's continuous with a career that started in armed
anti-colonial underground activity in the 1930s. He is CPI's most senior and most durable
parliamentary voice, arguing consistently from an anti-colonial, anti-capital position. His attack
in this debate fits a decades-long pattern; it is not a specifically 1957-crisis-driven argument.

Full biographical monograph (Lok Sabha Secretariat, Oct 1990):
`https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/58677/1/Eminent_Parliamentarians_Series_Bhupesh_Gupta.pdf`

## Shri B. R. Bhagat (Bali Ram Bhagat) — the Deputy Minister who moved the Bill and answered Gupta

**Indian National Congress, Bihar.** Born 7 October 1922. Joined Congress in 1939, active in the
independence struggle. Member, Provisional Parliament, 1950–52. **Deputy Minister for Finance,
1956–63** — meaning he was TTK's own deputy for the entirety of the FERA-permanence period, and this
Bill's floor management was squarely his job, not a peripheral assignment. Went on to a long
ministerial career (later Railways, External Affairs, Defence Minister under Indira Gandhi in the
1980s). Died 2 January 2011.

**What this explains:** Bhagat is not an independent voice weighing in — he is the government's own
man on this exact brief for seven straight years. His "industrialisation and developmental needs"
answer to Gupta is the settled institutional position of the Finance Ministry under TTK, not an
improvised rebuttal.

## Shri P. D. Himatsingka (Prabhu Dayal Himatsingka) — raised the technical objection about Sea Customs Act cross-reference

**Indian National Congress, Bihar** (Godda constituency in the Lok Sabha later, 1962–71; sat in the
Rajya Sabha earlier, including 1957 — the chamber and date of this debate). Born 16 August 1889,
Dumka, Bihar; died 2 June 1991, aged 101. The Himatsingka family name is associated with a
prominent Marwari business lineage (jute, later textiles — the modern Himatsingka Group is a
descendant enterprise), though the direct family link to this specific parliamentarian is not
confirmed by the sources checked here and should not be assumed without verification.

**What this explains, tentatively:** if the business-family association holds, it would explain his
close technical reading of the Bill's enforcement clauses (the s.23A/Sea Customs Act cross-reference
he presses TTK's ministry on) as the concern of someone attentive to how exchange-control
enforcement actually bites on commercial actors — but this is circumstantial, not confirmed. Flagged
as **unverified** rather than asserted.

## Shri Kishen Chand — pushed for even stricter enforcement than the Bill provided

**Likely Praja Socialist Party (PSP), Andhra Pradesh/Hyderabad region** — a "Kishen Chand" stood as
the **PSP candidate for Hyderabad in the 1957 Lok Sabha general election** (lost to Congress's
Vinayak Rao, 19,666 votes to 77,153). Separately, a "Kishen Chand" is cited elsewhere as an MP
active on press-regulation questions (Price-Page Schedule debates) in the same era, and a "Kishen
Chand" served later as **Lieutenant Governor of Delhi** in the Emergency period per one secondary
source. **These references have not been confirmed as the same person, and the RS 1957 speaker's
exact party affiliation is not independently confirmed here** — flagged as the least-certain
identification in this set.

**What would fit if PSP is correct:** PSP (Praja Socialist Party) combined non-Communist democratic
socialism with strong economic nationalism and suspicion of both foreign and domestic big capital —
consistent with his push in the debate for *stricter* regulation than the Government itself
proposed, and his focus on gold-smuggling and currency-note trafficking as concrete enforcement
failures rather than on the sterling/Britain question Gupta raised. **Confidence: this whole
identification is INFERRED, not established — treat with more caution than the other three.**
