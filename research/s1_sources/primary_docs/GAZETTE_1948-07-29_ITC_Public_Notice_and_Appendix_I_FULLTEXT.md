# Import Trade Control Public Notice, 20/26 July 1948 (with Appendix I — currency-area licensing policy table)

**Source:** Gazette of India Extraordinary, No. 45, published 29 July 1948, Ministry of Commerce.
Internet Archive identifier `in.gazette.e.1948.45`.
Route: `https://archive.org/cors/in.gazette.e.1948.45/E-2434-1948-0000-112111_djvu.txt`

This is the item flagged in the working notes as needed for "Appendix I transcription" —
the Ministry of Commerce's public notice governing import licences for July–December 1948,
issued days before the debates the project already holds (CAD Legislative, 9–13 August 1948,
on the sterling balances). It sets out, currency-area by currency-area, which imports would be
licensed for the second half of 1948, and appends the detailed policy table (Appendix I),
the licence-validity rules (Appendix II), and the new application form (Appendix III).

**OCR quality:** ABBYY FineReader scan of a 1948 letterpress original — moderate error rate,
typical of this project's Gazette captures (digit/letter substitutions throughout: "govemment"
for "government", "l(18)" for likely "1(18)", etc.). Prose is legible; treat any numeral or
proper name with caution and cross-check before quoting precisely.

**Key opening passages (clean enough to quote with light silent correction of OCR noise):**

> "Subject: governing the issue of import licences for the period July-December 1948... The
> following decisions made by the Government of India governing the issue of import licences for
> goods falling under Parts II to V of the Import Trade Control Schedule for the licensing period
> July-December 1948 are hereby published for general information."

> "As already announced on 3rd July 1948 the exchange position in respect of sterling and soft
> currency countries is now easier and restrictions on import of certain goods from these
> countries have been removed vide Open General Licence No. XI."

> "The foreign exchange position in respect of dollar and hard currency however continues to
> require a close watch as in the past."

Currency-area definitions given for the licensing period: **Dollar Area** (all of North, Central
and South America and the Philippines except Brazil, Chile, Uruguay); **hard currency**
(Bizonia/Anglo-American Zones in Germany, Belgium and colonies, Portugal and colonies excluding
Portuguese India, Japan); **medium currency** (Sweden, Switzerland — "neither hard nor soft").

> "4. Appended to this Public Notice is a statement (Appendix I) which shows in tabular form
> licensing policy which will be followed in respect of imports from different areas except
> Japan."

Administrative detail: licence validity six months from issue (one year for Appendix II
articles); a new application form (Appendix III); past-imports evidence required from any
completed financial year 1937-38 to 1947-48; separate procedure to follow for "new-comers."
Signed **G. R. Kamat, Jt. Secy.**, Ministry of Commerce.

**Why this matters for the essay:** this is the administrative machinery — the currency-zone
import licensing regime — running in parallel with, and largely independent of, the sterling
balances negotiations captured in the August 1948 Constituent Assembly debates. It corroborates
the IMF's 1957 India chapter's description of a similar (by then simplified to a two-way
soft/hard split) currency-zone licensing system, and gives the 1948 starting point for that
machinery's evolution across the decade — useful for tracing continuity between the licence-raj
apparatus described in FINDING 4 of `H1_INDIA_CHAPTER_FOUND_and_T1.5_RESOLVED.md` and its 1948
origins.

**No mention of Britain, sterling causation, or FERA anywhere in this notice** — it is entirely
Ministry of Commerce import-licensing administration, not Ministry of Finance exchange control.
