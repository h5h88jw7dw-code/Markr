# GATT L/648/Add.1 — India's statement on import restrictions, 6 August 1957 — FULL TEXT

**FOUND, complete, clean text (born-digital PDF, no OCR).**

**Source:** GATT document L/648/Add.1, "Import Restrictions of India - Statement by the Indian
Representatives at the Intersessional Committee Meeting on 6 August 1957," dated 09/08/1957.
Direct PDF: `https://www.wto.org/gatt_docs/English/SULPDF/90700170.pdf`

Found via the GATT document index at `https://www.wto.org/gatt_docs/l.htm`, which lists every
`L/`-series document with its date, title, and direct PDF link — a working, complete index that
resolves the project's outstanding "GATT 1957 India consultation" target. India's related 1957
filings in the same index: **L/615** (25 Jan 1957, "Indian Import Restrictions - Changes in the
Restrictions"), **L/648** (24 Jul 1957, "Import Restrictions of India - Notification of
Modifications" — the policy statement this document is an addendum to), and **L/673** (16 Sept
1957, tariff schedule rectifications, not substantively relevant).

## ⚠️ This is the single closest documentary link found anywhere in this project between "sterling
balances" and the 1957 restriction-tightening decision — read the nuance carefully.

> "1. The import policy of India for January-June 1957 and July-September 1957 has been framed
> against the background of a rapid decline in the foreign exchange reserves of the country.
> **Since the beginning of 1957 India's sterling balances, which constitute the country's foreign
> exchange reserve, have been declining at the rate of about Rs.10 million per day**, if drawals of
> Rs.950 million from the International Monetary Fund are left out of account. The overall level of
> these reserves was **Rs.4,317 million on 19 July 1957**, as compared to **Rs.7,353 million on 6
> January 1956** and **Rs.5,284 million on 4 January 1957**."

**Read this precisely.** "Sterling balances" here is used as accounting terminology — it describes
*what form India's own foreign exchange reserves were held in* (sterling, per the sterling-area
arrangement), not a claim that Britain restricted or withheld access to them. The document is
saying "our reserves, which happen to be sterling-denominated, are running out because of our own
trade deficit" — not "Britain is constraining us." This is a materially different claim from the
essay's original thesis (British/sterling-balance *pressure* causing FERA permanence), and it
should not be quoted without this distinction made explicit. It is, however, the clearest instance
found anywhere in the project of a government document using the word "sterling" in the same
breath as the reserve-decline reasoning that led directly into the restriction-tightening of
late 1957 — genuinely useful as long as the accounting-terminology point is not elided.

## The rest of the reasoning — entirely domestic, matching every other 1957 document in the project

> "2. This reduction in the reserves has been largely the result of **increased trade deficits**.
> The adverse trade balance during January-April 1957 was Rs.1,135 million, as against Rs.293
> million in January-June 1955, Rs.358 million in July-December 1955, Rs.991 million in
> January-June 1956 and Rs.1,116 million in July-December 1956. Imports during January-April 1957
> were at the half-yearly rate of Rs.4,893 million..."
>
> "3. In view of the increasing payments deficit and the declining foreign exchange reserves,
> intensification of import restrictions has been unavoidable. The restrictions have been imposed
> on balance-of-payments considerations."
>
> "6. As regards capital goods, the Government of India have no alternative but to restrict
> licensing to schemes which are covered by suitable deferred payment arrangements or by inflow of
> fresh foreign capital or by foreign assistance programmes. The essential needs of the economy for
> current purposes have to take precedence over the requirements for expansion."
>
> "8. Steps have been taken in the field of budgetary and monetary policy to reduce the pressure on
> the balance of payments. Excise duties on a number of commodities like sugar, tea, coffee,
> unmanufactured tobacco, matches, paper, petroleum products, cement and steel have been increased.
> **The Reserve Bank of India has raised the Bank Rate**, and commercial banks have been asked to
> follow a cautious lending policy."
>
> "9. The Government of India continue to explore the possibilities of securing additional foreign
> exchange resources. Further loans have been obtained from the International Bank for
> Reconstruction and Development, and as mentioned above, **a considerable amount has been
> borrowed from the International Monetary Fund**."

**No mention of Britain, the United Kingdom, or the sterling balances agreements anywhere in the
document.** The causal chain given is entirely domestic and multilateral: trade deficit → reserve
decline → restriction, financed by IBRD and IMF borrowing, not by any UK facility.

## Confidence, verdict, what it changes

**Confidence tag:** STATED — India's own official submission to GATT, contemporaneous (6 August
1957, three weeks before FERA permanence passed the Lok Sabha on 31 August).

**Verdict:** this is the reserve-decline mechanism the essay needs numerically, dated precisely,
and in the government's own words — but it is a **balance-of-payments** story, not a **British
constraint** story, even though it is the one document in the project that puts "sterling" and
"1957 restrictions" in the same sentence. The essay should use this document for the *numbers*
(Rs.4,317m reserves at 19 July 1957 against Rs.7,353m eighteen months earlier — a ~41% fall) and
for the *timing* (this statement predates FERA permanence by three weeks and the RBI Ordinance by
nearly three months), while being precise that "sterling balances" is doing accounting work here,
not causal work.

**Cross-check:** these reserve figures corroborate and refine H7 (TTK's May 1957 Budget speech
estimate of "a little below Rs.500 crores" ≈ Rs.5,000 million, falling "Rs.5 to 6 crores a week").
This document's Rs.4,317 million at 19 July 1957 sits consistent with that trajectory, and gives an
even more precise, GATT-audited figure for the same period.
