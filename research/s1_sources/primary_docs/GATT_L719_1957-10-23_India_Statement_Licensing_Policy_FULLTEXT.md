# GATT L/719 — India's statement on import licensing policy, 23 October 1957 — FULL TEXT

**FOUND, complete, clean text. This is the single most precisely-dated document recovered in this
project relative to the RBI Ordinance.**

**Source:** GATT document L/719, "CONTRACTING PARTIES - Twelfth Session - Indian Import
Restrictions - Statement by the Government of India on the Licensing Policy for the Period October
1957 and March 1958," dated 23 October 1957. Direct PDF:
`https://www.wto.org/gatt_docs/English/SULPDF/90710030.pdf`. Found via the GATT document index,
`https://www.wto.org/gatt_docs/l.htm`.

**This document is dated eight days before the RBI Ordinance No. 6 of 1957 (31 October 1957)** —
the closest-dated primary source to that event found anywhere in the project, and it states the
statutory breach in the government's own words, in real time.

## ⚠️ The key passage — this closes T1.7/H7's arithmetic with a precise, contemporaneous, official figure

> "During January/June 1957 **the sterling balances which constitute the foreign exchange reserves
> of India** had declined rapidly and had fallen by **Rupees 766 million**. During the period the
> Government of India had also utilized in addition a credit of Rupees 950 million from the
> International Monetary Fund. During July/September 1957, the reduction in the reserves amounted
> to Rupees 1,000 million. **The reserves today stand substantially below the minimum prescribed by
> law, namely Rupees 4,000 million which the Reserve Bank is required to maintain. The law permits
> Government to give a temporary relaxation under which the Reserve Bank can permit its external
> balances to decline to Rupees 3,000 million subject to a time limit of twelve months.**"

**This is the statutory floor from T1.7 (Rs 400 crore = Rupees 4,000 million, set by Act 38 of
1956) and the temporary-relaxation mechanism from the RBI Ordinance (cutting the floor to Rs 300
crore = Rupees 3,000 million for twelve months) — described by the Government of India itself,
eight days before the Ordinance was promulgated, as already breached ("reserves today stand
substantially below" the Rs 4,000 million minimum).** This is a materially better source than the
project's earlier arithmetic reconstruction (H7, extrapolating from TTK's May 1957 speech) — it is
the Government's own contemporaneous statement of the breach, not an inference from a rate of
decline.

Again, note the same accounting usage of "sterling balances" as in L/648/Add.1 (6 August 1957): it
identifies the *form* the reserves are held in, not a claim of British constraint.

## The rest of the statement — reserves, imports, and the October–March policy

> "The main reason for the decline in reserves has been the increase in imports which rose from
> Rupees 7,506 million in 1955/56 to Rupees 10,765 million in 1956/57..."
>
> "In these circumstances the Government of India would have been fully justified in making the
> import policy for October 1957 to March 1958 even more restrictive than the one in force during
> July/September 1957. **In actual fact the October-March policy is in many respects very much more
> liberal than the policy in the immediately preceding quarter.**"
>
> On capital goods: "Import licenses for capital goods for setting up new industrial projects will
> be subject to the strictest scrutiny and except for relatively small projects... new import
> licences will only be granted if the project is covered by external investment in one form or
> another."
>
> On non-discrimination: "It will be recalled that in Indian import licensing a distinction is made
> only between the dollar area and the rest of the world which is treated as the soft currency
> area. The discrimination between dollar and non-dollar sources has been reduced steadily to the
> maximum extent **having due regard, however, to the sterling area's payments position with the
> dollar area since India belongs to the sterling area.**"
>
> On other measures being taken: "(a) Anti-inflationary measures have been taken and the latest
> budget has not only raised excise duties but has introduced a taxation on wealth and expenditure.
> (b) Credit policy has been severely tightened. (c) Efforts are being made to raise credit abroad
> from institutions like the World Bank and elsewhere. (d) Greater emphasis is laid on export
> promotion though the efforts of the Government in this direction necessarily depend upon the
> co-operation and policies of India's trading partners."

**No mention of Britain or the United Kingdom by name anywhere in the document.** "Sterling area"
appears once, in the context of preserving the sterling area's collective payments position with
the dollar area when calibrating India's own dollar-vs-soft-currency licensing split — a
multilateral, mechanical point about which currency zone gets favoured, not a claim of British
pressure on India specifically.

## Confidence, verdict, what it changes

**Confidence tag:** STATED — official Government of India submission to the GATT Twelfth Session,
contemporaneous to the week.

**Verdict:** this **upgrades H7 from an arithmetic estimate to a documented fact**. H7 inferred,
from TTK's May 1957 Budget speech ("a little below Rs.500 crores," falling "Rs.5 to 6 crores a
week"), that the statutory floor would be breached in roughly 17–22 weeks. This document states
outright, eight days before the Ordinance, that the breach had already happened: "reserves today
stand substantially below the minimum prescribed by law." The temporary-relaxation figure it cites
(decline to Rs 3,000 million / Rs 300 crore, twelve-month limit) matches the RBI Ordinance's actual
mechanism precisely — meaning the Ordinance was not a sudden emergency measure but the codification
of a relaxation the Government was already describing as necessary and lawful under existing
statute, three days before this statement... err, eight days before it was formalised.
