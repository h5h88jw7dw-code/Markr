**Verdict against acceptance criterion:** the acceptance criterion is essentially failed, but as a **found negative**, not as "not attempted." Nobody moved to substitute a date for the deleted duration clause. The Bill's central move — permanence rather than another fixed-term extension — went through **without a single amendment being tabled against it**, from either side of the House. The closest thing to opposition was Pandit Thakur Das Bhargava's closing procedural complaint that the whole Bill (all 19 clauses) was "rushed through" in one sitting without time for proper scrutiny or a Select Committee reference — a complaint about process and speed, not a substantive objection to permanence itself, and not an amendment.

**What this changes:** the brief's hoped-for upgrade — "renewal was a live option, evidenced by an actual amendment record rather than inference" — **does not materialise**. If anything this cuts the other way: the fact that not even a token amendment proposing (say) a further five- or ten-year extension was moved, by any member including the more skeptical ones (Nayar, Bhargava, Mukerjee, Masani — all of whom spoke and are quoted or referenced in the T1.3a file), suggests permanence was not seriously contested as a *policy* question on the floor at all — the debate's energy went entirely into the adjudication/penalty provisions (Clauses 6, 7, 13, 16, 19), not into the duration question. The essay should not claim "renewal was a live option debated and rejected" — the accurate claim is "permanence was proposed and passed unopposed and unamended; the debate's actual contestation was entirely about enforcement powers, not about whether to keep a sunset clause."
