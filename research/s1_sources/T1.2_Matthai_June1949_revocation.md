# T1.2 — John Matthai on the June 1949 revocation

### T1.2 — PARTIAL (upgraded from NOT FOUND)
**Route that worked:** Firecrawl scrape of "The Problems of Plenty, 1947–56" (`rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/content/PDFs/90037.pdf`) — a chapter hosted directly on the Reserve Bank of India's own document server. This is almost certainly the RBI's own official institutional history (G. Balachandran's volume, the same source the brief already treats as the existing citation for this event) — but reached here as a directly-fetched primary institutional publication, not a secondhand paraphrase of it. Full text saved at `primary_docs/RBI_History_Problems_of_Plenty_1947-56_FULLTEXT.md`.

**Verbatim extract:**

> "By spring 1949, India's external payments position was beginning to get out of hand. It had virtually exhausted the second (flexible) sterling drawing of £40 million intended originally for the second year and its application to draw a further $100 million from the IMF was encountering stiff American resistance. **Recognizing the need to bring about a better fit between sterling releases and imports, John Matthai, who had meanwhile replaced Chetty as Finance Minister, revoked all Open General Licences (OGLs) for soft currency imports in June 1949 while pressing for a further release from the blocked account to finance imports in the pipeline.** The latter request and those for sterling releases over the next two years formed the subject of talks between the two countries in London in July 1949."

This is more precise than anything previously held on this event: it specifies **exactly what was revoked** (Open General Licences for *soft*-currency imports — not, as earlier framing in this project loosely assumed, a "dollar import licence") and the **stated institutional reason** (to fit the pace of import licensing to the pace of sterling releases actually being granted by Britain, which by spring 1949 had fallen badly out of sync with import commitments already made). It also directly links the June revocation forward to the July 1949 London talks and the resulting Wilson–Menon agreement (already sourced independently via Abreu for T1.1), closing the loop between T1.1 and T1.2: the Cripps memorandum (21 April 1949) → Matthai's OGL revocation (June 1949) → the London talks (July 1949) → the Wilson–Menon settlement (August 1949) is now a single, continuously sourced sequence.

**Confidence tag:** STATED for the fact, date, and scope of the revocation (a specific, named regulatory action — "revoked all Open General Licences (OGLs) for soft currency imports" — dated to June 1949) and for the immediate causal chain to the July 1949 talks. Still **CONVERGENT, not STATED**, for the brief's original, stronger ask: this is the RBI's institutional historian's account of Matthai's action, not a verbatim quotation of Matthai's own words (a press note, Gazette notification, or floor statement in his own voice has still not been located).

**Verdict against acceptance criterion:** partially met. The brief wanted "an Indian minister's own statement of the reason" to convert this from CONVERGENT to STATED. What was found instead is a much more precise and directly-sourced institutional account (RBI's own official history, fetched directly rather than through paraphrase) — a real upgrade in precision and directness, but not quite the specific upgrade requested.

**What this changes:** the essay can now describe the June 1949 action precisely (OGLs for soft-currency imports, not "dollar imports" loosely stated) and can present it as the middle link in a fully continuous and now well-sourced causal chain running from the Cripps memorandum through to the August 1949 settlement.
