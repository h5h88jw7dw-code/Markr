# T1.1 — The Cripps memorandum of April 1949, and the British reserve position

### T1.1 — FOUND
**Route that worked:** Firecrawl scrape of Marcelo de Paiva Abreu, "India as a Creditor: Sterling Balances, 1940–1953" (PUC-Rio Discussion Paper No. 643 — open-access working-paper companion to Abreu, "Britain as a debtor: Indian sterling balances, 1940–53," *Economic History Review* 2017) — `econ.puc-rio.br/api/uploads/adm/trabalhos/files/td643.pdf`. This is a secondary source citing primary UK National Archives files at the file level — flagged accordingly, not cited as though the TNA file itself was consulted directly.

**Verbatim extract:**

> "In the midst of a rise of Indian imports, India's spent £90 million of sterling balances in the first 9 months of 1948-9, £50 million by drawing on no. 1 account and £40 million by anticipating the 1948-9 release. **A Cripps memorandum was handed to Pandit Nehru. It stressed that this pace could not be maintained, proposed a tapering off of Indian drawings and mentioned difficulties surrounding the supply of capital goods.** The Indian answer was very angry as Mathai, the new Finance minister, thought the British inconsiderate for delaying a negative answer to Indian wishes for ten weeks and thus allowing further Indian drawings. The British were on the defensive and decided not to answer in the same 'belligerent tone'. Crucially, it was decided 'to depart from the idea that releases have been in anticipation of those agreed for the future'."

Footnote 86, giving the file-level archival citation for the memorandum itself:
> "Memo on Rate of expenditure of India's sterling balances, enclosed to Rowan [Second Secretary, H.M. Treasury] to Cripps, 21 April 1949, **TNA:T236/4412** and Turnbull [Private Secretary to the Secretary of State for India] to Rowe-Dutton, 21 Jan. 1948. India Office. L/E/9/303."

Footnote 87, showing the Treasury/CRO correspondence trail continued into May 1949 (i.e. this was a live, sustained concern, not a single isolated memo):
> "India's Import Policy TNC ON (49) 154, 4 May 1949, and tel. 1576 CRO to High Commission India 14 May 1949, **TNA:T238/59**."

The August 1949 resolution (Wilson–Menon agreement), confirming the memorandum's warning was acted on within months:
> "In August 1949, Harold Wilson, President of the Board of Trade, and Krishna Menon, Indian High Commissioner in London, signed a new agreement covering £81 million of advanced releases and defining releases of £50 million in 1949-50 and £50 million in 1950-1. The 'obnoxious dollar ration was done away with'."
