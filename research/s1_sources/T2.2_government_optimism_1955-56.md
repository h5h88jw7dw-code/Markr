# T2.2 — Government optimism about the exchange position, 1955–56

### T2.2 — FOUND. The contrast the brief hoped for is real, and stronger than expected.

**Verbatim extracts — the optimism, in the Finance Minister's own words, eighteen months before TTK's "ad infinitum":**

**Paragraph 23 — the feared deficit did not materialise:**
> "Honourable Members will recall that when we started the First Five Year Plan we anticipated a large deficit in our overall balance of payments. **But it has not materialised to the extent we originally feared.** Due very largely to the great increase in our food production, which has resulted in a very substantial reduction in our food imports, as well as to the aid we have received from foreign countries over these years, **the total reduction in our sterling balances during these five years has been about Rs. 150 crores only.** Lately, the improvement in the domestic economy has made itself felt somewhat in our external trade. **The country's balance of payments on current account for the first nine months of 1955 showed a surplus of Rs. 25 crores and the year as a whole may show a surplus of about Rs. 35 crores** as compared with a deficit of Rs. 4 crores in the previous year."

**Paragraph 24 — the sterling balances were *rising*:**
> "The level of our sterling balances reflects the overall balance of payments. **At the beginning of the year 1955, these stood at Rs. 731 crores and at the end at Rs. 735 crores.** That they have risen only by Rs. 4 crores as against the much greater surpluses on current account is due to a substantial deficit in capital account."

And on the IMF liability being nearly extinguished:
> "…India repurchase[d] … obligations from the International Monetary Fund to the extent of Rs. 22.2 crores in 1954. **A further sum of Rs. 19.3 crores was repurchased during 1955, reducing India's liability to the Fund to about Rs. 6 crores only.**"

**Paragraph 25 — India was a net *contributor* to the sterling area's central reserve, and was *liberalising* dollar imports:**
> "**Our dollar position has also improved.** During the first three quarters of 1955 there was a substantial surplus on current account of Rs. 31 crores as against Rs. 3 crores in the corresponding period of 1954. As a result of the improvement in the dollar balance of payments, **India made a net contribution of $53 millions to the Central Reserve in 1955 as compared with a net withdrawal of $15 millions in 1954. During the year further progress was made towards liberalisation of dollar imports. This is in line with the policies followed generally by members of the Sterling area.** Further reduction of the discrimination against dollar imports or liberalisation of dollar payments will obviously depend on the overall position of the Central gold-dollar reserves."

**Paragraph 27 — the caveat, which is forward-looking and conditional, not a report of present distress:**
> "**Despite the satisfactory payments position at present**, the projected increase in the pace of development in the country will necessarily put a heavy s[train]… we [may not] be able to achieve any surplus in our external accounts but we are also likely to be faced with fairly substantial deficits. These deficits may be greater than they otherwise would have been because we have to contend with certain adverse factors. Tea prices have fallen and export trade in our other major items, jute and cotton textiles is becoming increasingly competitive… Our import requirements on the other hand must necessarily continue to rise as the tempo of our development programme increases."

**Paragraph 30 — the sterling-area link described as healthy and voluntary:**
> "**The Government of India have continued, as in the past, to maintain their close economic link with the Sterling Area.** India was represented at the Commonwealth Finance Ministers' Conference in Istanbul in September last when common problems facing the Sterling Area were discussed and the need for the continuance of sound internal economic policy was recognised. The Conference also reviewed the difficulties that had been facing sterling in the recent past and determined on measures to restore its position in the markets of the world."

**Confidence tag:** STATED (Government's own published text).

**Verdict against acceptance criterion:** met, fully. The brief (annotation A19) asked: *"If the Second Plan documents or the 1955–56 / 1956–57 budget speeches expected the shortage to ease, the contrast with TTK's ad infinitum eighteen months later is devastating and dates the belief's hardening precisely."* The February 1956 Budget speech does not merely "expect the shortage to ease" — it reports that **the shortage was not occurring**: a current-account surplus, sterling balances rising, the IMF liability nearly repaid, the dollar position improved, dollar imports being *liberalised*, and India a net *contributor* of $53 million to the sterling area's central reserve.

**What this changes — the belief's hardening can now be dated to an eighteen-month window, with primary sources at both ends:**

| Date | Source | Position on the exchange constraint |
|---|---|---|
| **Feb 1956** | Deshmukh, Budget speech 1956-57 (official text) | "Satisfactory payments position at present." Surplus, balances rising, **liberalising** dollar imports, net contributor to the Central Reserve. Deficits are a *projected future* consequence of the Second Plan. |
| **May 1956** | Deshmukh, SOR to RBI Amendment Bill 33 of 1956 (Gazette) | Reserve requirements "place a serious limitation" on funding the Second Plan; "foreign exchange reserves are **likely to decrease**" — still forward-looking. |
| **31 May 1957** | B. K. Nehru to US State Dept (FRUS Doc. 166) | "$650 million of reserves [used] since April, 1956"; reserves will hit "their legal minimum" before year-end. |
| **13 July 1957** | TTK, SOR to FERA Bill 47 of 1957 (Gazette) | Shortage "likely to continue for an **indefinite period**"; "difficult to visualise… any foreseeable future" without exchange control. |
| **31 Aug 1957** | TTK, Lok Sabha | "Shortage of foreign exchange is likely to continue **ad infinitum**." |
| **31 Oct 1957** | Presidential Ordinance 6 of 1957 | Currency-reserve floor cut by emergency decree; RBI "cannot comply" with its own law. |

**Eighteen months separate "satisfactory payments position at present" from "ad infinitum."** Both statements were made by Finance Ministers to Parliament; the second man (Krishnamachari) had been sitting in the House when the first was made. Nothing in either the 1956 Budget speech or the 1957 SOR attributes the change to Britain, sterling-area membership, or the sterling balances — in February 1956 the sterling-area link is described as an asset being maintained "as in the past," and India is a *creditor* to the pool.

**A caution for the essay:** this finding cuts against a strong "Britain caused it" reading as much as it supports the "belief hardened suddenly" reading. In early 1956 India was liberalising dollar imports *in line with sterling-area policy*, not straining against it. The proximate cause visible in every primary document recovered in this project is the Second Five Year Plan's import surge, not British pressure. The sterling-balances constraint is very well evidenced for **1948–50** (see T1.3, H6, B1/B3); it is **not** the reason the Government itself gives for the 1956–57 reversal.
