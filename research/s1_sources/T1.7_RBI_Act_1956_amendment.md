# T1.7 — The Reserve Bank of India Act currency-backing change, October 1956

### T1.7 — FOUND, WITH AN IMPORTANT DATE CORRECTION
**Route that worked:** Firecrawl scrape of the RBI Act 1934 as currently amended (`indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/9176/1/a1934-2reserve.pdf`), reading the amendment footnotes attached to each substituted sub-section — these give the exact amending Act number and commencement date for every change, including the ones now superseded.

**Verbatim extract:**

Section 33(1)-(2) (the Issue Department's asset-backing requirement — this is "the" minimum reserve system provision):
> "**33. Assets of the Issue Department.—**(1) The assets of the Issue Department shall consist of gold coin, gold bullion, [foreign securities], rupee coin and rupee securities to such aggregate amount as is not less than the total of the liabilities of the Issue Department as hereinafter defined.
> (2) The aggregate value of the gold coin, gold bullion and foreign securities held as assets and the aggregate value of the gold coin and gold bullion so held shall not at any time be less than two hundred crores of rupees and one hundred and fifteen crores of rupees, respectively."

Footnote to sub-section (2): **"Subs. by Act 48 of 1957, s. 2, for sub-section (2) (w.e.f. 31-10-1957)."**

Footnote to "foreign securities" in sub-section (1): "Subs. by Act 62 of 1948, s. 7 and the Schedule, for 'sterling securities' (w.e.f. 1-1-1949)" — i.e., the word "sterling" was itself only removed from the statute in 1949, replaced by the generic "foreign securities."

By contrast, everywhere **Act 38 of 1956** actually appears in this Act's amendment history, it is dated **6 October 1956** and touches *different* sections:
> "Ins. by Act 38 of 1956, s. 2 (w.e.f. 6-10-1956)" — inserting words into section 17 (the Bank's business/functions clause, "to carry out its exchange, remittance and other banking operations").
> "Subs. by Act 38 of 1956, s. 4, for section 37 (w.e.f. 6-10-1956)" — substituting section 37 (Suspension of assets requirements as to foreign securities — the emergency-override provision allowing the Bank, with Government sanction, to temporarily hold less than the required foreign-securities minimum).
> "Subs. by Act 38 of 1956, s. 5, for sub-section (1)/(3)... (w.e.f. 6-10-1956)" — amending section 42 (cash reserves of scheduled banks to be kept with the Bank, i.e. the statutory CRR mechanism).

**Confidence tag:** STATED (all of this is read directly from the Act's own consolidated text and its footnoted legislative history, the most primary source available short of the Gazette notification of the Bill itself)

**Verdict against acceptance criterion:** partially met, with a correction that changes what the essay should claim. The brief's framing was: *"If India amended its own currency-backing law in October 1956 to release reserves for the Plan, and ran out anyway within a year, that is a state with no remaining options."* The evidence found does **not** support dating the currency-backing (minimum-reserve-floor) amendment itself to October 1956. What **is** dated to 6 October 1956 (Act 38 of 1956) are: the Bank's general business/exchange-operations clause (s.17), the *suspension mechanism* for the foreign-securities requirement (s.37 — i.e., the emergency escape hatch, not the requirement itself), and the scheduled-bank CRR provisions (s.42). The actual **rupee floor of the minimum reserve system** — Rs 200 crore total, of which Rs 115 crore in gold, which is the number usually associated with "the 1956 minimum reserve system" in secondary/popular accounts (banking-exam prep sites, etc.) — was fixed by a **different Act, No. 48 of 1957, effective 31 October 1957**, i.e. a full year later, and only weeks before the Foreign Exchange Regulation (Amendment) Bill's own August 1957 debate concluded.

**What this changes.** This is a genuine correction to a commonly repeated but apparently imprecise secondary-source claim (WebSearch's own synthesis, likely reflecting banking-exam-prep sites, states flatly "In 1956, the Proportional Reserve System was replaced by the more flexible Minimum Reserve System" — the primary Act text does not straightforwardly support that date for the specific reserve-floor figures). Two readings are possible and need to be distinguished before the essay uses this fact:
1. **The essay's intended point may survive, just needs its date fixed to 1957, not 1956** — if so, this is actually a *stronger* exhibit than the brief realised: the Rs 200cr/Rs115cr floor was fixed by statute in the same calendar year as the FERA permanence debate (Oct 1957), not the year before, tightening the "no remaining options" timeline rather than loosening it.
2. **Or Act 38 of 1956 did do something substantively relevant** (the s.37 suspension-mechanism amendment is plausibly about giving the Bank more flexibility to breathe below the foreign-securities floor during exactly this kind of crisis) **that just isn't "the currency-backing change" as originally framed** — in which case the essay's claim should be rewritten around s.37's 1956 flexibility mechanism, not a 1956 change to the floor itself.

Either way, **the essay's current wording (implying a straightforward "1956 amendment changed the backing system, and India ran out within a year regardless") needs to be re-checked against these two Acts before publication** — it currently conflates two different amending Acts a year apart.
