**Verbatim extract:**

On the 1955 date — Hansard, HC Deb 01 March 1955 vol 537 cc1865-7 (full text saved at `primary_docs/Hansard_1955-03-01_Transferable_Sterling.md`):
> Mr. R. A. Butler: "I have nothing to add to the statement which I made on Thursday, 24th February."
> Mr. Gaitskell: "Does the right hon. Gentleman not realise that the intervention of the Exchange Equalisation Account in the market for transferable sterling, in the manner stated last Thursday, really amounts to convertibility of the £ by the back door? ... any non-resident or foreigner holding sterling is now able to convert it into dollars at the expense of our reserves, while we and other countries of the sterling area still have severe restrictions on dollar imports?"

This dates the Bank of England's decision to support the transferable-sterling rate to **Thursday, 24 February 1955** (the statement Butler refers to), with this 1 March 1955 exchange being the first parliamentary scrutiny of it. Gaitskell's phrase "convertibility of the £ by the back door" is the primary-source origin for characterising Feb 1955 as *de facto* non-resident convertibility.

On the 1958 date — per WebSearch synthesis of secondary sources (Naef, *An Exchange Rate History of the United Kingdom*, Ch. 6; Bank of England dealers' reports):

> Bank of England dealers' report, 29 December 1958: "the first day of convertibility found markets a little confused at the start but later there was considerable activity."
