# T1.5 — The Import Trade Control Handbook of Rules and Procedure

> # ⚠️ SUPERSEDED — **T1.5 IS NOW ANSWERED, AND IT IS ANSWERED AGAINST THE ESSAY.**
>
> Everything below this box is the *obsolete* "OPEN / not answered" record from an earlier pass. Retained as an elimination log only.
>
> **The question is settled.** Full reasoning: `H1_INDIA_CHAPTER_FOUND_and_T1.5_RESOLVED.md`. Source text: `primary_docs/IMF_8th_Annual_Report_1957_INDIA_CHAPTER_FULLTEXT.md`.
>
> T1.5 asked whether a foreign-exchange release under FERA was **a separate discretionary decision** from an import licence, or **automatic on production of one** — the brief's own acceptance criterion being: *"If separate, the 'FERA is the binding gate' paragraph is proven. If automatic, that paragraph must be cut."*
>
> The IMF's *Eighth Annual Report on Exchange Restrictions* (1957), describing India's system as at end-1956, states under **Imports and Import Payments**:
>
> > "**Where a valid import license is held, the required exchange is released by authorized banks on presentation of the exchange control copy of the license.**"
>
> **It was automatic.** The release was performed by authorised commercial banks, not by the Reserve Bank exercising discretion, and it was triggered by presentation of a document. There is no second gate. The only reserved discretion is a narrow exception for *advance* payment before shipping documents arrive — a timing concession, not a gate on the transaction.
>
> **Consequence for the essay: the "FERA is the binding gate" paragraph must be cut**, per the brief's own stated criterion. The binding constraint on imports was the **licence**, issued by the Chief Controller of Imports and Exports under a half-yearly quota policy announced in the "Red Book" — not the FERA exchange release.
>
> This also retrospectively vindicates the elimination of *East India Commercial Co. v. Collector of Customs* recorded below: that case turned on import-licence **end-use conditions**, and licences turn out to be exactly where the binding constraint actually lived.
>
> The Handbook itself was never retrieved in full and no longer needs to be — the question it was wanted for has been answered by a better source.
