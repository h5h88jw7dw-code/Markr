## Verbatim — the complete statement

The item is headed, at printed p. 387:

> **STATEMENT RE RELEASE OF STERLING BALANCES**

> **The Minister of Finance (Shri C. D. Deshmukh):** With your permission, Sir, I wish to make a brief Statement on the arrangement recently ag[reed] with the Government of the United Kingdom for releases from our sterling balances.
>
> **The House is aware that the present Sterling Balances agreement entered into in July [1]948 will terminate on the 30th June, [1]95[1]. As a result of my recent discussions with Mr. Gaitskell, the Chancellor of the Exchequer in the United Kingdom Government, the two governments have now agreed on the broad outlines of a new agreement to be formally concluded in due course. The contemplated agreement will provide for a release up to £35 millions from our Sterling Balances in each of the six years [commencing] with the 1st July, [1]95[1]. Provision will be made for a measure of flexibility by stipulating that any part of the amount not drawn in a particular year will be carried forward and added to the releases for later periods. Further, the two governments will consult together if in any year the Government of India find it necessary to exceed the release of £35 millions and to draw upon the releases for subsequent periods by more than £5 millions.**
>
> **The latest balance in our Sterling Balance is £[1]69 millions. Of this a certain amount will be utilised under the present agreement before the 30th June, [1]95[1], to meet the probable deficit in our balances and payments over the next few months. A part of our Sterling Balance will have to be maintained over a long period as a currency and exchange reserve.**
>
> **Having regard to all these considerations, the Government of India are of the view that the proposed scale of releases over a period of six years is in the best interests of the country.**

**And — significantly — the House was not permitted to debate it:**

> **Shri Hussain Imam (Bihar):** Sir, would it be open to the House to discuss this agreement? Will some day be allotted for its discussion? **It is a very important financial arrangement which the House would like to discuss.**
>
> **Mr. Deputy-Speaker:** On prior occasions such Agreements were discussed. What does the Leader of the House say?
>
> **The Prime Minister and Minister of External Affairs (Shri Jawaharlal Nehru):** **We shall consider the matter, Sir, whether it should be discussed in the House or not.**
>
> *The House then adjourned till a Quarter to Eleven of the Clock on Friday the 8th December, [1]950.*

## Confidence, verdict, and what it changes

**Confidence tag:** STATED (Finance Minister's own statement on the floor, official report, recovered verbatim via IA OCR).

**Verdict against acceptance criterion:** **met.** H8 asked for the 1951 six-year agreement's own terms, "confirm[ing] the £35m × 6 schedule and the terminal date; this is the hinge of the essay's Act IV." All of it is here, from the minister who negotiated it, four days after the negotiation: £35 million a year, six years, commencing 1 July 1951, unused amounts carried forward, consultation required to exceed £35m by more than £5m in any year.

**This independently corroborates the RBI institutional history** (T2.1), which gave the same terms secondhand. The agreement is now sourced to the primary parliamentary record, not to the Bank's later account of it.

**Three things here are new to the project:**

1. **The counterparty is named: Hugh Gaitskell**, Chancellor of the Exchequer. The negotiation was minister-to-minister, and the announcement came four days before the Select Committee reported on the RBI (Amendment) Bill (13 Dec 1950).

2. **A residual-balance figure at December 1950** — "The latest balance in our Sterling Balance is £[1]69 millions." **The OCR digit-dropping makes this figure unsafe as printed.** £69m is implausibly low against the ~£800m+ range documented elsewhere for 1949–51 (Abreu's table gives India at £796m in June 1951); the likely true reading is **£1,169 million** or similar with a dropped leading digit. **Do not quote this number without verifying against the printed volume.** The *prose* around it is reliable and is the useful part.

3. **The agreement was announced to Parliament but not debated.** Hussain Imam asked directly for a day to discuss "a very important financial arrangement"; Nehru's answer — "We shall consider the matter, Sir, whether it should be discussed in the House or not" — is a deferral, and the House adjourned. This is a small but real finding for the essay's argument about how the sterling settlements were handled procedurally: the six-year agreement that governed India's access to its own reserves until June 1957 was **communicated to the legislature in a four-paragraph ministerial statement, with a request for debate left unanswered.** It parallels the 1957 FERA permanence passing in a single sitting (T2.3) and Chetty's 1948 complaint (U10) that six-monthly renegotiation made coherent policy impossible.

**A note on the terminal date, which matters for T2.1.** Deshmukh states the six years commence 1 July 1951, which puts expiry at **30 June 1957** — matching the RBI history's account and the date already established in `T2.1_No2_Account_June1957.md`. The agreement therefore expired **eight weeks before** TTK moved FERA permanence on 31 August 1957, and four months before the RBI Ordinance of 31 October 1957.
