## Unplanned breakthrough: this also closes most of T2.10 (the Symons 1972 TNA memorandum)

While reading de Bromhead et al. for the Kennedy citation, their paper turned out to **independently cite and quote the exact same restricted document flagged in the brief as T2.10** — TNA T267/29, Symons, "Sterling balances since the war," 1972 — which the brief says is "Not digitised... Hussain has already read it (her note 130 cites p. 78), so her footnotes are the cheaper route." De Bromhead et al. give a **second, independent citation route into this document**, with **different page references (pp. 96–98)** and a clear paraphrase of its argument:

> "A more direct distributional channel relates to specific cases of dissatisfied members. Symons, in a confidential 1972 historical memorandum restricted to the British Treasury, **argued that India's experience with blocking led to its subsequent diversification.** This claim can be tested by applying dummy variables to countries in our sample which had **sterling balances blocked: Ceylon, India, Pakistan, and Sudan.**"
> Footnotes: "TNA, T267/29, Symons, 'Sterling balances since the war', 1972 (hereafter, TNA, Symons)." / "TNA, Symons, pp.96-8."

This is directly on point for the essay's central causal claim (blocked sterling → policy consequences) and is now STATED via a second, independent peer-reviewed source (published *Economic History Review* 2023, not just Hussain's unpublished note), with a specific page range (96–98) that is different from and additional to Hussain's (p. 78) — meaning between the two secondary citations, at least pp. 78 and 96–98 of the restricted 1972 memorandum are now known, quoted-through-intermediary, content, without needing the undigitised file itself.

A related, precisely dated fact from the same paper, useful for the essay's broader chronology: **India crossed the 50%-of-reserves-in-sterling threshold (its formal "reserve transition") in September 1966** — "Like Singapore, India (reserve transition September 1966) made a deliberate financial decision to diversify, anticipating sterling devaluation." This gives a hard endpoint for how long India's sterling exposure actually persisted after the 1957 Act.

**What this changes:** T2.10 can very likely be marked FOUND (via intermediary — de Bromhead et al. and Hussain both citing TNA T267/29) rather than requiring a TNA Discovery catalogue check, unless a verbatim quotation (not paraphrase) of Symons's own words is specifically required — de Bromhead et al.'s sentence is a paraphrase of Symons's argument, not a direct quotation, so if the essay wants Symons's own words rather than two independent scholars' summary of them, the underlying file would still need consulting. But as a CONVERGENT-to-STATED causal claim (two independent peer-reviewed/scholarly citations of the same restricted document, agreeing on its argument), this is now well-sourced enough to cite directly in the essay with an "as summarised in de Bromhead et al. (2023), p.X, citing TNA T267/29, Symons (1972), pp.96–8" formulation.
