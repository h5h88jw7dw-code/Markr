# The Basic Travel Quota and the reserve constraint — consolidated evidence

*Extracted from `T2_Evidence_Comb_Findings.md` (Findings 6, 9, 10) for use as a standalone
evidence packet. This is the chain that gives S1 Classic a human-scale consequence wired to its
own proven mechanism, without borrowing anything from Track 2.*

---

## The claim this evidence supports

**Defensible version:** the foreign exchange an ordinary Indian could take abroad was rationed as a
direct function of the national reserve position — the same reserve position that Classic's Lock A
(RBI Act minimum reserve floor) and Lock B (IMF gold-subscription formula capping unconditional
drawing rights) already constrain. When the reserve position collapsed at the turn of 1956–57, the
personal travel allowance was not reduced. It was **abolished**.

**Version NOT supported, and to be avoided:** that FERA was the "chief enabler" of the
Licence-Permit Raj, or that a continuous instrument called the Basic Travel Quota runs from 1947 to
1997 ratcheting downward. Neither is evidenced. See the confidence ladder at the end.

---

## Link 1 — The allowance was indexed to the reserve position. STATED, 1946–51.

**Source:** *History of the Reserve Bank of India*, Vol. 1 (RBI's official history), Ch. 20,
"Exchange for Foreign Travel, etc.", pp. ~659–60.

> "To facilitate such visits, relaxations were made in the exchange control restrictions on foreign
> travel, including the abolition in February 1946 of the Exit (Finance) Permit system. **In
> September 1946, the Reserve Bank was authorised to deal with all applications for purposes of
> travel for business without reference to Government.** The rules for release of exchange for
> journeys abroad for medical treatment and for education were also liberalised in the immediate
> post-war period. **Foreign exchange was also granted for visits abroad for reasons of personal
> convenience, but the amounts varied according to the overall foreign exchange position and the
> degree of softness of the currencies of the countries visited.** In these as also in matters
> relating to release of exchange for emigrants, the policy of the Bank of England was followed
> closely."

**This is the load-bearing sentence:** amounts "varied according to the overall foreign exchange
position." The RBI, describing its own practice, in its own official history.

⚠️ **Do not suppress the last sentence.** "the policy of the Bank of England was followed closely"
supports *British in design* — which this project has established many times over — and does **not**
support *British in 1957 causation*.

## Link 2 — Travel was the named exception to free release, and personal travel required individual application. STATED, end-1956.

**Source:** IMF, *Eighth Annual Report on Exchange Restrictions* (1957), India country chapter,
"Payments for and from Invisibles." (`primary_docs/IMF_8th_Annual_Report_1957_INDIA_CHAPTER_FULLTEXT.md`)

> "In general, payments abroad for invisibles require approval. However, **except for travel,
> insurance, and a few other items, foreign exchange is granted freely** for such payments…"

> "Foreign exchange facilities are granted for business travel and education abroad according to **a
> scale of allowances fixed for each country. To obtain exchange for travel for other purposes,
> individual application must be made.**"

**Why this matters beyond the BTQ:** set beside the import rule — exchange released by banks
"**without approval of Reserve Bank**" on production of the licence (RBI *Exchange Control Manual*
7A.1(i)) — these two sentences establish the trade/person asymmetry as **stated fact**, not
inference:

| | Trade (imports) | Persons (travel) |
|---|---|---|
| Default | released by banks, **no RBI approval** | approval **required**; a **named exception** to "granted freely" |
| Business / education | — | fixed **scale of allowances** per country |
| Personal / other | — | **"individual application must be made"** |

The case-by-case discretionary gate existed. It stood in front of the traveller, not the importer.

## Link 3 — On 1 January 1957 the allowance went to zero. STATED.

**Source:** same IMF chapter, "Changes during 1956" chronology.

> "**December 27.** It was announced that **beginning January 1, 1957 there would be no basic
> allowance of foreign exchange for travel abroad for pleasure or personal convenience.**"

Three things to note:

1. **The name.** The Fund calls it a "**basic allowance** of foreign exchange for travel abroad" —
   the direct lexical and functional ancestor of the "**Basic** Travel Quota."
2. **The value.** Not squeezed. **Nil**, for pleasure and personal-convenience travel.
3. **The date.** 1 January 1957 — **eight months before** FERA permanence passed the Lok Sabha
   (31 August 1957), and inside the same eight-week reversal at the turn of 1956–57 during which
   India went from *liberalising* dollar dealing (3 September 1956) to restriction.

---

## Link 4 — Statutory basis: FERA 1947, resolved by date

A measure in force on **1 January 1957** cannot rest on the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act **1973**,
which did not exist. The basic allowance therefore operated under **FERA 1947** — the same statute
whose 1957 permanence is the essay's subject. **Confidence: STATED** (by necessity of date).

This matters: it means the human consequence attaches to the *right* FERA, not the 1973 successor.

## What would strengthen this further

- **The earliest *figure*.** This packet has the abolition (1 Jan 1957) but not a rupee/dollar
  amount for the allowance before it. AREAER editions for **1955** and **1956** would give the
  pre-abolition scale. The 1955 edition is at
  `https://www.elibrary.imf.org/downloadpdf/display/book/9781475548662/9781475548662.pdf`
  (391pp, 33.5MB) — browser download only; it defeats automated retrieval on file size alone.
- **The restoration date.** When the basic allowance came back after 1 January 1957 is unknown here.
  The AREAER Ninth (1958) and Tenth (1959) editions would show it.
- **RBI later history volumes** (1951–67, 1967–81) for the instrument's renaming to "Basic Travel
  Quota" and the lineage this packet marks INFERRED.
