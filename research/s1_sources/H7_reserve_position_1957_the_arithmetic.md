# H7 (and the completion of T1.7 / T2.2) — the 1957 reserve position, and why the floor had to move

### FOUND — via TTK's own Budget speech of May 1957, and it closes the causal loop numerically.

**Route that worked:** `https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/bspeech/bs195758.pdf` — Budget speech for **1957-58 (FINAL), delivered May 1957 by T. T. Krishnamachari**. Official Government text, not OCR. Full text saved at `primary_docs/BudgetSpeech_1957-58_TTK_OFFICIAL_TEXT.md`.

This is the **missing middle** of the project's timeline: it sits between Deshmukh's optimistic February 1956 Budget (T2.2) and TTK's "ad infinitum" of August 1957 (T1.3a), and it is by the same man who would move FERA permanence three months later.

---

## Verbatim — the reserve position

**Paragraph 11, under the heading FOREIGN EXCHANGE:**
> "**The major problem before the country is the large and continuing strain on foreign exchange resources.** Since the beginning of the fiscal year, 1956-57, there has been a persistent pressure on the balance of payments resulting in a draft on the country's foreign exchange resources of about Rs.300 crores. Since, during this period, a credit of Rs.60.7 crores was obtained from the International Monetary Fund, the fall in the Reserve Bank's holdings amounted to about Rs.240 crores."

**Paragraph 12 — the decisive figures:**
> "The balance of payments for the first quarter of 1956-57 showed a current account deficit of Rs.44.5 crores; the deficit widened to Rs.81.4 crores in the second quarter. For the third quarter for which data are now available, there was a further increase in the deficit which worked out at Rs.84.8 crores. Excluding 'official donations' which enter into the current account, the deficits for the three quarters were Rs.54.1 crores, Rs.89.9 crores and Rs.92.4 crores. Since January, 1957, further foreign exchange resources of the order of Rs.86 crores have been used up. **The foreign assets of the Reserve Bank now stand a little below Rs.500 crores. Despite the measures already taken to restrict imports, there is no indication yet of an abatement in the rate of drawals which averages Rs.5 to 6 crores a week.**"

**Paragraph 13 — the trade gap, and a NEW LEAD:**
> "On a rough estimate, imports for the year, 1956-57 will aggregate to over Rs.1000 crores, while exports will be around Rs.650 crores. The bulk of the additional imports received during the year is for developmental purposes and should help strengthen the economy in due course. It is, however, obvious that foreign exchange expenditure has to be limited to the availability of resources, and the present imbalance in the country's external account rectified. **The various corrective measures adopted during the year were reviewed in the March White Paper**; further action in the same direction which is necessary will, as I have just said, be taken."

**Paragraph 14 — no cushion left:**
> "**There is hardly any cushion left in our foreign exchange reserves to permit a further significant draft on them.** The level of imports hereafter has necessarily to be regulated in the light of our current earnings and the inflow of supplemental resources we can secure from abroad and should be closely related to essential requirements of the high priority projects in the Plan. The import policy for the second half of 1957-58 is being formulated with the objective of securing a further sizeable saving on imports."

**Paragraph 10 — the monetary consequence:**
> "…the increase in money supply… occurred in spite of the large balance of payments deficit which resulted in a **precipitous fall in the Reserve Bank's foreign exchange holdings**."

## The arithmetic that closes the loop

Put the May 1957 figures against the statutory floor documented in the Gazette (see `GAZETTE_1956-05-02_RBI_Amendment_Bill_33_of_1956_SOR_FULLTEXT.md` and `GAZETTE_1957_RBI_Ordinance_6_and_Bill_80_currency_reserve_SOR_FULLTEXT.md`):

- **Statutory floor set by Act 38 of 1956 (in force 6 Oct 1956):** Rs **400 crore** in foreign securities (plus Rs 115 crore in gold), with s.37 suspension available only down to a **Rs 300 crore** hard proviso.
- **Reserve Bank's foreign assets, May 1957 (TTK's own figure):** "a little below **Rs.500 crores**."
- **Rate of drawal (TTK's own figure):** "**Rs.5 to 6 crores a week**," with "no indication yet of an abatement."

At Rs 5–6 crore a week, a fall from just under Rs 500 crore to the Rs 400 crore statutory floor takes roughly **17 to 20 weeks**. Counting from the speech in May 1957, that lands in **September–October 1957**.

**The Ordinance was promulgated on 31 October 1957.**

The Government did not amend the currency-backing law as an abstract act of planning flexibility. It amended it — by Presidential Ordinance, with Parliament not in session, "necessary for him to take immediate action" — at almost exactly the week the Finance Minister's own published arithmetic said the Reserve Bank would breach the statutory minimum. TTK's SOR of 8 November then says so in plain words: *"it is not possible for the Reserve Bank to comply with the requirements of section 33(2)."*

## Confidence and verdicts

**Confidence tag:** STATED for every figure above (Government's own published Budget speech; the statutory figures from the Gazette).

**Verdict against H7's acceptance criterion:** **partially met, and arguably superseded.** H7 asked for "the residual blocked sterling balance at 30 June 1957 — sizes the counterfactual (how much of the 1957 gap India's own money would have covered)." That specific figure is still not recovered. But what has been recovered is arguably the more useful quantity for the essay: **the total reserve position and its rate of decline, from the Finance Minister, in the same months** — Rs ~500 crore falling at Rs 5–6 crore/week, against a Rs 400 crore floor, with a Rs 350 crore annual trade gap (imports >Rs 1000 crore vs exports ~Rs 650 crore). Any counterfactual about blocked sterling has to be sized against *these* numbers.

**A significant negative finding: the word "sterling" appears ZERO times in the entire 1957-58 Budget speech.** In a speech of some 78,000 characters devoted substantially to the foreign-exchange crisis, by the minister who would make exchange control permanent three months later, there is no reference to sterling, sterling balances, the sterling area, or Britain as a cause. "Foreign exchange" appears 21 times; "balance of payments" 6 times. Combined with the same absence in the FERA Bill's Statement of Objects and Reasons (13 July 1957) and in TTK's Lok Sabha speech (31 August 1957), this is now **three independent 1957 documents by the same minister, none of which mention sterling.**

## The "March White Paper" — identified (and it is less exotic than it first appeared)

TTK refers at paragraph 13 to "the March White Paper" in which "the various corrective measures adopted during the year were reviewed." **This has now been identified: it is the White Paper on the Budget**, the standard document circulated alongside the Budget, not a special crisis paper. TTK introduced it in his Interim Budget speech of 19 March 1957 (recovered — see `primary_docs/BudgetSpeech_1957-58_INTERIM_TTK_19March1957.md`):

> "The White Paper on the Budget which is being circulated separately attempts to give a review of major economic developments during the year. It is, therefore, not necessary for me to cover the whole ground over again."
> "White Paper mentions the various measures we have taken in the last few months to bring the situation under control, and I have every hope that these measures will prove effective in due course."

And in the final Budget speech he refers back to it: "In the March White Paper the various measures taken to save and conserve foreign exchange have been listed."

So it is a **real, dated, citable Government document of March 1957 that itemises the exchange-conserving measures** — still worth retrieving, since it would give the fullest official list of the corrective measures (import cuts, credit controls) taken before the FERA permanence decision. But it is a routine budget document, not a previously unknown crisis white paper, and it should not be described as the latter. Likely location: the Budget papers for 1957-58 (`indiabudget.gov.in` archive, or eParlib alongside `eparlib.nic.in.230`).
